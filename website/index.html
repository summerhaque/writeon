Why it matters

The best way to learn to read is to write.

Reading well means understanding the ingredients of a story and how language fits together. Writing your own story is how those ingredients start to make sense.

But a public school teacher can have around 150 students. Coaching each of them on their own sentences, in real time, is practically impossible.

AI can do what an overwhelmed classroom can’t: give every student just enough guidance to keep moving, and never so much that it drowns out their voice.

And it’s not only about reading scores. Writing is a place to be creative and to say what you feel. It builds confidence, resilience and a sense of self.

The nine ingredients

Start anywhere.

Every story has the same nine ingredients. Write On! lays them out as a path, with progress that fills in as you go.

The hardest part of writing is starting, so kids can open whichever stage excites them first. A character already in your head? Start there. A strange place? Start there.

How it works

From a first idea to a real book.

01

Choose your adventure.

Fantasy, sci-fi, mystery, comedy or friendship. Answer a few quick questions, and the coach builds a story roadmap made for that story.

02

Work through the stages, in any order.

Each stage asks the questions real writers ask. What does your hero want more than anything? What stands in the way? Everything saves as you type.

03

Practise on your own sentences.

Mini-lessons on similes, hyperbole, word choice, sensory detail, dialogue and show, don’t tell. Each one ends with a line the student wrote, rewritten, next to the original.

Design a cover and write the About the Author page. Write On! exports a professionally formatted PDF that lays out stories of any length, and the book lands on the Story Shelf.

Keep going.

The AI writing coach

Hints, not answers.

When a student is stuck or turns in a draft, Coach Sparkle reads the writing and answers like a good writing teacher. First a question, then a sharper hint, then an example from someone else’s story. It explains ideas like protagonist and antagonist, suggests plot twists, and helps with revision. It never writes the story for them.

It builds vocabulary too, with new words and new ways to say the same thing.

For teachers and parents

Built for the way writing is taught.

The writing process, in any order.
The nine stages follow the process students already learn in school: prewriting, drafting, revising and publishing. Students start wherever they’re ready.
Hints, not answers.
When a student asks for feedback, their writing is sent to an AI model that is prompt-engineered to ask questions and give hints, not to produce a paragraph. What a student hands in is theirs.
Nothing gets lost.
Work autosaves as students type, so a story can be picked up again tomorrow, right where they left it.
Something to hold at the end.
Every story can end as a printed book with the student’s name on the cover, and a place on the Story Shelf.
