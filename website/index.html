The blank page

The hardest part is starting.

Ask a kid to write a story and watch the pencil hover. Most writing tools don’t help. They begin at “Once upon a time” and expect you to march forward from there.

Summer Haque built Write On! the other way around. A story has nine stages, and you can open any of them first. Got a character in your head? Start there. A strange place? Start there. Nothing yet? The app hands you a spark.

How it works

From first idea to a real book.

01

Pick a kind of story.

Fantasy, sci-fi, mystery, comedy or friendship. Answer three quick questions and Write On! draws a roadmap for that story: where to begin, and a first prompt for every stage.

02

Work through each stage.

Every stage is a short workspace with real writer’s questions. Characters asks what your hero wants more than anything, and what they’re afraid of. Setting asks what you can hear and smell. Everything saves as you type.

Stage 2 of 9

Characters

Name
Mira
What does she want more than anything?
For the kids at her new school to think she’s interesting.
What is she afraid of?
That if anyone finds out the real her, they’ll think she’s boring.

03

Practise on your own sentences.

Six short lessons: similes, hyperbole, word choice, sensory detail, dialogue, and show, don’t tell. Each one ends with a sentence you wrote, rewritten, next to the original.

Show, don’t tell

Before

Leo was angry.

After

Leo slammed his locker so hard it bounced back open.

We see what Leo does, so we feel his anger without being handed the word.

04

Publish a real book.

Design a cover, write the About the Author page, and Write On! binds it into a PDF book with a title page, chapters and pictures. Kids add the app’s scene plates or a photo of their own drawing. Then the book goes on their shelf.

The coach

A coach that hands the pen back.

Coach Sparkle sits beside every stage. It asks the questions a writing teacher asks. When a kid is stuck, it gives a hint, then a sharper one, then an example from somebody else’s story. Three hints. Never an answer.

Try it. Ask it to write the story for you.

Fantasy · Characters · Hints, never answers

For teachers and parents

Built for the way writing is taught.

The writing process, in any order.
Prewriting, drafting, revising, publishing. The nine stages follow the process students already learn in class. They can just start where they’re ready.
Nothing writes for your students.
Coach Sparkle is scripted to ask questions and give hints. It can’t produce a paragraph. What a student hands in is theirs.
Private by design.
No accounts to manage, no ads, no tracking. Stories stay on the device they were written on.
Something to hold at the end.
Every story can end as a printed book with the student’s name on the cover. That’s the part they show their families.

Who made it

Made by Summer Haque.

Summer is a student who built Write On! for the Congressional App Challenge. One design decision came first: the hardest part of writing is starting, so in Write On! you can start anywhere.

Everything else follows from that. A coach that asks instead of answers. Lessons that work on your own sentences. And an ending you can hold in your hands.
